Animation examples

Replays of two recorded runs: SP1's fuel offload at fixed payload and Phase 2's silo screening. Each comes with the command that made it and the caveats it has to be read with. Every replay page here is the command's own output with the site's frame and three drawing patches of the replay template added when the site is built (site/build.py, REPLAY_TEXT_FIXES; the template is frozen for SP2); the three pages that also carry editorial additions (SP1.1, SP1.2 and 2.1) say in their entries what was added and where; 2.2 and 2.3 carry none. Every frame is drawn from the run's recorded time series; nothing is re-simulated in the browser or in the videos.

  • concept A: vertical silo
  • planar 2-D, rotating Earth
  • sweep-optimized, unthrottled
  • 200 km circular, 28.5°, due east
  • gate vehicle, +14.3% calibration miss

What these runs are, and what they are not

The examples come from two shipped runs, each made from a clean commit (per its metrics.json): SP1's results/silo_offload_2d/20261003T112934Z (git b3150c1754ee) and Phase 2's results/silo_screening_2d/20260930T175743Z (git 7ad381f227a9). Both cover concept A only: a vertical silo whose drive imposes a prescribed acceleration. Every finding they illustrate is preliminary and lives in docs/findings/.

Phase 2 asked how much payload the push adds to an unchanged rocket. SP1 asked it the other way round: at the pad's payload and orbit, how much stage-1 propellant can the pushed rocket leave out? Both answers are differences between runs of one vehicle model, not figures for a real Falcon 9.

Read before quoting any number on this page

  • Planar point-mass model over a rotating spherical Earth, with a generic CD(Mach) table. Guidance is sweep-optimized, not optimal control. Nothing is throttled, so max-Q and q-alpha are upper bounds, and the pitch kick has no angle-of-attack aerodynamics.
  • The gate vehicle calibrates +14.3% high: 26,054.4 kg to a 200 km orbit against the published 22,800 kg, outside the ±10% band. It is a documented miss, accepted on 2026-09-30 (CAL-f9-leo-2d). Read the numbers as differences between runs.
  • No structural mass is charged for the push: the stack feels 4.0 g on the track. In payload terms, about 8.1 t of extra stage-1 structure would cancel silo_cold's whole gain (a linear extrapolation); in propellant terms, about 8.5 t (extrapolated) cancels SP1's whole offload. Neither is a sized structure: no structural model exists yet.
  • The drive is a prescribed 3 g acceleration with no force or power limit, a massless carriage and no air drag in a vented shaft. Under this drive the release speed, the payload and the offload do not depend on carriage mass, drive efficiency or shaft drag: they move only drive energy and power. The massless carriage and the missing shaft drag bias those low (the shaft drag by about 0.04% in drive energy and 0.08% in peak power and interface force: an own estimate in RQ3-2d from q = 3.6 kPa at the 76.7 m/s exit, not a measured value). Under it a hot start cannot add exit speed.

SP1 · run 20261003T112934Z

SP1: fuel offload at fixed payload

  • experiment silo_offload_2d
  • run 20261003T112934Z
  • git b3150c1754ee, clean
  • pre-registered (c2a4cf0; Amendment 1, d336933)
  • preliminary, 2026-10-03

The question: with the payload and orbit held at the pad's, how much stage-1 propellant can a silo-pushed rocket leave out? The offloaded rocket flies with its tanks partly filled and every dry mass unchanged, so the comparison is between different vehicles carrying the same payload to the same orbit. Every number below is from RQ1-fuel-offload-2d, the README's "Fuel replaced at fixed payload (SP1)" and the run's summary.md.

The headline, and what sits beside it

At the pad's payload (26,054.4 kg to a 200 km circular orbit) the 3 g, 100 m cold-start silo lets the gate vehicle leave out 41.26 t of stage-1 propellant: 10.04% of the 410.9 t stage-1 load, 7.96% of the 518.4 t total (silo_cold_s1; sweep-optimized and unthrottled, before any structural mass).

  • Calibration. The gate vehicle calibrates +14.3% high. On the README-loads fork, which calibrates inside the band (+8.3%), the same case removes 36.01 t, 9.10% of its stage-1 load. Read the headline as 9 to 10% of stage 1 across the two vehicle models, not as a Falcon 9 figure.
  • Structure. Nothing is charged for the 4.0 g full-stack push. An assumed +2, +4 and +8.1 t of stage-1 dry mass leave 32.29, 22.88 and 1.98 t; about 8.5 t (extrapolated) leaves nothing.
  • Push or lighter stack? The pad flown with the same 41.26 t offload falls only 1,402.0 kg short of the payload. Read as the pre-registration worded it (that shortfall against the offload, 3.4%), most of the offload is the lighter stack's higher thrust-to-weight, not the push. A delta-v reading chosen after the run gives the lighter stack 28 to 29% of the ideal delta-v saved, the release speed and the head start's trajectory effects 64 to 66%, and the pad's hold-down convention 6 to 7%. The findings note reports both (SP1.2).
  • Loads. Max-Q is 3.4% above the pad's, where the full-load silo flew 16.0% below it; q-alpha at the kick is 3.0× the pad's. Nothing throttles or limits either.
  • Stage 2. The stage-2 offload case failed its independent verification. Stage 1 is the only headline.
  • Energy. The 12.4 t of RP-1 removed carry about 128× the push's 1.16 MWh of metered electricity in combustion heat. That is a ratio of unlike quantities, not an efficiency claim or a substitution rate: it leaves out LOX production, fuel supply, grid losses and the facility, and falls to 5.65 with the assumed +8.1 t of structure.
  • Model. Sweep-optimized guidance, no throttle, a pitch kick with no angle-of-attack penalty, a prescribed drive, and partly filled tanks of an unchanged vehicle (not one redesigned for the smaller load).

SP1.1 · the headline offload

Pad against the offloaded silo

Two different rockets carrying the same 26,054.4 kg to the same 200 km orbit: the fully fuelled pad baseline, and the same vehicle with 41.26 t less stage-1 propellant pushed out of a 100 m silo.

What it shows

pad lights stage 1 two seconds before release with a 2 s thrust ramp while held down, burning 2.7 t on the hold-down, then lets go. silo_cold_s1 starts 41.26 t lighter (tanks partly filled, every dry mass unchanged), is pushed 100 m up the shaft at 3 g net (2.6 s, felt 4.0 g, 20.81 MN at the interface), leaves the mouth at 76.7 m/s and lights 0.5 s later with the same 2 s ramp. Both reach the same orbit with the same payload; silo_cold_s1 reaches MECO 12.8 s sooner.

Numbers: RQ1-fuel-offload-2d (headline table and figures) and the run's summary.md. Max-Q is unthrottled, an upper bound.

For the hypothesis

  • 41.26 t of stage-1 propellant left out (10.04% of stage 1, 7.96% of the total). The pad's own control removes nothing at this payload, and an independent payload search on the offloaded vehicle returns the pad's payload to within 0.0085 kg.
  • It is 2.76× the ideal-screening estimate (14.98 t), and the loss breakdown explains the difference, so it is not treated as a bug. Of the 228.2 m/s of ideal delta-v the offloaded vehicle does without: release speed 76.7 m/s (34%), lower gravity and steering loss 124.7 m/s (55%), the pad's hold-down burn 14.4 m/s (6%), back-pressure 13.3 m/s (6%), drag −0.9 m/s.
  • Under every ±10% sensitivity arm (stage-1 dry mass, Isp, CD, drive efficiency) it stays between 38.67 and 44.46 t.

Against it, stated as plainly

  • No structural mass is charged for the 4.0 g push. Each tonne of assumed stage-1 strengthening takes 4.5 to 5.1 t off the offload; at +8.1 t only 1.98 t (0.48% of stage 1) remains, and about 8.5 t (extrapolated) takes all of it.
  • Read as the pre-registration worded it, most of the offload is the lighter stack's higher thrust-to-weight, which the push makes usable but does not produce. Read in ideal delta-v, an interpretation chosen after the run, the lighter stack is 28 to 29% of it (SP1.2).
  • Part of it is a baseline convention: 14.4 m/s of the 228.2 m/s (6.3%) is the pad's clamped hold-down burn, which a silo lit after release does not pay.
  • The max-Q benefit is spent: the full-load silo's max-Q was 16.0% below the pad's; offloaded, it is 3.35% above it. q-alpha at the kick is 226.9 against the pad's 74.7 Pa rad, and the model charges nothing for it.
  • The figure depends on the vehicle masses: 9.10% of stage 1 (36.01 t) on the README-loads fork against 10.04% here.
  • The facility: 1,156.4 kWh of electricity per launch at an assumed 50% drive efficiency, a 1.597 GW peak drive power, and 160 m long including 60 m of braking.

SP1.2 · head start or lighter stack?

The offloaded silo against its paired pad

Numbers: RQ1-fuel-offload-2d, "Head start or lighter stack? The paired pad", and the run's summary.md. Max-Q is unthrottled and compared with the full pad's 37.19 kPa; each gravity loss is at its own run's payload.

What it says

  • The push is needed to carry the offload: without it the same tanks carry 1,402.0 kg less payload. At equal stack mass the head start is worth 1,402.0 kg, 93.5% of the 1,498.8 kg it is worth on the fully fuelled stack.
  • Read as the pre-registration worded it, the "small margin" branch fires: 1,402.0 kg is 3.4% of the 41,262.9 kg offload, so most of the offload is the lighter stack's thrust-to-weight, not the head start. The findings note states that verdict but does not adopt it, because it compares kilograms of payload with kilograms of propellant.
  • Read in ideal delta-v, an interpretation chosen after the run: of the 228.2 m/s the offload removes, the release speed is 76.7 m/s (34%), the head start's trajectory effects at equal stack mass 69 to 74 m/s (30 to 33%), the pad's hold-down convention 14 to 16 m/s (6 to 7%), and the lighter stack's own thrust-to-weight gain 63 to 67 m/s (28 to 29%). The two orders of a two-step split bracket each range.
  • Offloading without a push raises max-Q far more: the paired pad's is 19.8% above the full pad's, the offloaded silo's 3.35%.

Read it with

  • Since SP2 step A1a replay itself labels silo_cold_s1__pad "(paired pad)", says it flies its own payload capacity (24,652.4 kg, 1,402.0 kg short of P_ref) and measures a payload change, not propellant saved. The copy here differs from the command's own output only by the site's frame, three drawing patches of the replay template (site/build.py, REPLAY_TEXT_FIXES; the template is frozen for SP2) and editorial sentences added to the page text: to the comparison caveat the paired pad's capacity and shortfall restated with the pre-registered reading of it (3.4% of the offload, so most of it is the lighter stack's thrust-to-weight; the delta-v reading gives it 28 to 29%); to the structure caveat the +8.1 t and 8.5 t sentence; to the subtitle what the two runs are and that the absent baseline is the full-load pad; to the drive caveat the size of the shaft-drag bias (REPLAY_PAGE_FIXES).
  • The delta-v split is a difference between runs, and one order of it mixes two payloads (24,652 kg and 26,054 kg), so it is bracketed, not exact.
  • The paired pad is held down while its engines ramp up, the same baseline convention as the full pad's, and the free kick, missing throttle and sweep-optimized guidance apply to both runs.

Pad against cold-start silo

The same rocket flown to a 200 km circular orbit twice: from the pad, and pushed out of a 100 m silo at 3 g net before its engines light.

What it shows

pad lights stage 1 two seconds before release with a 2 s thrust ramp while held down, then lets go. silo_cold is pushed 100 m up the shaft at 3 g net (2.6 s) and leaves the mouth at 76.7 m/s; stage 1 lights 0.5 s later with the same 2 s ramp. Both fly the same guidance family, each with its own sweep-optimized settings, and each carries its own payload capacity P*.

RunP* (sweep-optimized)vs pad
pad (baseline)lit at −2 s, 2 s ramp, held down26,054.4 kg—
silo_cold3 g net over 100 m, release at 76.7 m/s, lit 0.5 s after release, 2 s ramp27,553.2 kg+1,498.8 kg (+5.75%)

Numbers: RQ3-silo-screening-2d, RQ6-aero-2d-preliminary and the README's Results so far.

For the hypothesis

  • The gain is about 2.0× the ideal rocket-equation screening estimate (765.4 kg). The loss breakdown explains the difference: a vertical release cuts gravity, steering, back-pressure and drag losses, and the pad burns 2.7 t while clamped. So it is not treated as a bug.
  • Under every ±10% sensitivity case the gain stays between +1,345 and +1,667 kg.
  • Unthrottled max-Q falls 16.0% (31.24 against 37.19 kPa).

Against it, stated as plainly

  • No structural mass is charged for the 4.0 g full-stack push (22.5 MN at the interface). About 8.1 t of stage-1 strengthening would cancel the whole gain.
  • Part of the headline is the baseline convention: the pad's clamped 2 s ramp accounts for 83.1 kg net. Against a pad lit at release the gain is +1,415.8 kg.
  • The calibration miss inflates it. On a vehicle calibrated to 22.8 t the gain would be expected near 1.3 t (derived by proportion, not run).
  • The trajectory part of the gain (34%) appears only after MECO and is unverified beyond the gravity-turn family. It is the part a better pad ascent (optimal control, roadmap Phase 5) could change, in either direction.
  • q-alpha at the kick is above the pad's (129.9 against 74.7 Pa rad), and the model charges nothing for it.
  • The facility is sized by power: a 1.725 GW peak at release, 2.249 GJ per launch (1,249.5 kWh of electricity at an assumed 50%), and 160 m long including 60 m of braking.

2.2 · RQ2, hot or cold start

Ignition timing: four ways to light stage 1

The same 3 g, 100 m push with four different stage-1 startups, to see what lighting the engines late costs.

Open the interactive replay MP4 1.1 MiB GIF 1.1 MiB

What it shows

All four leave the silo at 76.7 m/s after 2.6 s; they differ only in when and how stage 1 reaches full thrust. silo_instant (dashed in the replay) steps to full thrust at release. No engine can do that, so it is a yardstick, not a design.

RunP*vs padvs silo_cold
silo_instantstep to full thrust at release (unphysical yardstick)27,880.4 kg+1,826.0 kg+327.1 kg
silo_coldlit 0.5 s after release, 2 s ramp27,553.2 kg+1,498.8 kg—
silo_hot_ramp_on_tracklit on the carriage at −2 s, ramp ends at release; 2,697.5 kg burned on the track27,788.2 kg+1,733.8 kg+235.0 kg
silo_cold_laglit 0.5 s after release, first-order lag, τ = 1 s27,553.2 kg+1,498.8 kg−0.04 kg

Numbers: RQ2-ignition-timing-2d. The pad baseline (26,054.4 kg) is not drawn in this replay.

What it says

  • Lighting after release is expensive in 2-D. silo_cold gives back 327.1 kg (34.74 m/s) of the instant start's +1,826.0 kg: 18% of it, and 2.37× what the 1-D formula g (td + tr/2) predicts.
  • A first-order lag of τ costs as much as a ramp of 2τ. silo_cold_lag and silo_cold tie (34.747 against 34.742 m/s).
  • Lighting on the carriage, with the ramp ending at release, is the best physical variant in this set: +235.0 kg over the cold start, paid for with 2.7 t of propellant burned on the track.
  • Lighting for the whole push (silo_hot_full, not drawn here) does not pay: 10.2 kg behind the cold start, and −18.5 to +0.1 kg under the ±10% cases. That is a tie, not a gain.

Read it with

  • Under the prescribed-acceleration drive a hot start cannot add exit speed. It buys drive energy and peak power instead, and only if the exhaust does not push on the carriage (the impingement fraction is unknown). The hot-start question waits for the force- and power-limited linear_motor of roadmap Phase 3.
  • silo_instant and silo_hot_ramp_on_track have no ±10% run, so the 327 kg loss and the +235 kg advantage carry no sensitivity.
  • The lag's startup shape differs from the pad's 2 s ramp, so its gain over the pad is not like for like; its loss against silo_instant is.
  • Structure is not charged in any of them. For silo_cold, about 8.1 t of stage-1 strengthening would cancel the gain (184 kg of payload per tonne of stage-1 dry mass, a linear extrapolation of silo_cold's ±10% dry-mass cases). That slope was measured only on silo_cold.

2.3 · RQ3, the abort case

Failed ignition: the push with no engines

What happens to a fully fuelled stack pushed out of the silo at 76.7 m/s when stage 1 never lights.

Open the interactive replay MP4 631 KiB GIF 662 KiB

What it shows

silo_failed gets the same 3 g, 100 m push as silo_cold and is never lit. The replay accepts a run that ended in impact: it draws the coast up and back into the shaft mouth beside silo_cold's ascent and reports no payload for it.

Numbers: RQ3-silo-screening-2d, RQ3-silo-screening-1d and the README's failed-ignition row.

Read it with

  • No abort is modelled. The full stack comes back to the shaft mouth at 76.6 m/s, 0.4 m from the axis.
  • In 1-D, unless the carriage is withdrawn within about 14.8 s, the stack first hits its own carriage, parked at its braking distance 60 m above the mouth, at 14.83 s and 68.6 m/s. That was not re-computed in 2-D.
  • A tilted exit as an abort mode is a planned Phase 3 item; nothing here tests it.
  • silo_failed did not reach orbit, so it has no payload capacity, no payload change and no screening check (status not_checked; the summary still lists the screening estimate at its release speed). Its loss budget covers only the unpowered coast (drag loss 0.146 m/s).

Reproduce

Permission first. The repository is public to read, but no license is granted. Cloning it and running the simulator is a use the license does not cover, so ask the copyright holder for written permission first (through GitHub, @khoks). The steps below are for the author and for anyone who has that permission.

Only each run's summary.md is tracked in git (SP1's run, Phase 2's run); the time series the replays need stay local. On a fresh clone, run the experiment first, then point replay or animate at the new timestamped directory it prints. A run made today is a new run at a newer commit, not the b3150c1 or 7ad381f runs shown here, so compare its summary.md before reusing these numbers. The user manual covers both commands in full.

uv sync

SP1, example SP1.1 (offload runs must be named with --runs):

uv run python -m launchsim run experiments/silo_offload_2d.yaml --no-sensitivity
uv run python -m launchsim replay results/silo_offload_2d/<new timestamp> --runs pad silo_cold_s1 --out pad-vs-silo-offload.html

Phase 2, example 2.1:

uv run python -m launchsim run experiments/silo_screening_2d.yaml --no-sensitivity
uv run python -m launchsim replay results/silo_screening_2d/<new timestamp> --runs pad silo_cold --out pad-vs-silo-cold.html

SP1's run solves eleven offload cases, pad controls in three modes and a paired pad (and eight offload sensitivity arms, which --no-sensitivity skips), so it takes far longer than Phase 2's. There a searched 2-D run takes about 7–10 s for the pad and 25–28 s for the lag variants, so the whole experiment takes a few minutes, and --variant silo_cold is enough for example 2.1. Output names ending in _replay.html, _animation.mp4 or _animation.gif are ignored by git.
