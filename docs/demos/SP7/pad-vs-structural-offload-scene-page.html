Launch Scene

T+0.00 safter release
Event marks over the time slider.

Keys: Space plays or pauses; [ and ] go to the previous and next event; Left and Right step 1 s, with Shift 10 s.

The launch scene is drawn on a canvas, which this browser does not show. The telemetry and the event list on this page carry the same recorded values as text.

Two runs play on one clock (time after release) and one camera: while both runs fly, the views share scale and pan (the same ground line and launch site), and each marks the other panel's vehicle with a hollow ring, named in a key on its view. A panel before its run's first row holds that row; a panel whose run has ended keeps its last view and scale bar under a banner, with no ring. The main view is to one true scale (except the position marker that replaces the vehicle once its body is under the pixel threshold, marked on the view, the markers of the flown path, of max-Q, of the separated bodies and of the other vehicle, and the shaft rings, never drawn under 2 px): the site, the vehicle, the shaft, carriage and rails, or the pad mount and clamps, drawn from configs/display and the page's drawing constants (drawn, not computed); the ground is Earth's curved surface. The flown path is the recorded one; the spent stage and the fairing halves follow display-only drag-free coasts (dashed), not model output; while one of them is within a few pixels of its vehicle, its marker is drawn over the vehicle's with a label on a leader. The max-Q mark is the run's recorded max-Q (its metrics), not a logged event. The close-up has a scale of its own: the stack shown is drawn at one fixed length, so its scale changes at staging and at the fairing drop (eased over a few seconds: before MECO toward the stage-1 engines, so the plume is seen to go out, then up to stage 2 after the separation; before the fairing drop toward the halves, held while they open and until they have left the close-up); it is not the view's scale. The camera frames the part of the view right of the close-up's column, so the close-up never covers the site, the flown path or its marks; when the panels are narrow and the canvas tall enough, the readout and the gauges sit in a band under the view. The gap opening at staging and the fairing halves opening there are drawn, not computed. Position, speed, the engines' vacuum thrust, attitude while the model defines one, and propellant are replayed from the recorded time series; nothing is integrated in the browser.

Events

Each event at the time of its matched row; select one to jump to it. Events marked synthesised are made from the metrics, not logged by the model (the caveats say how).

Telemetry

Speed is relative to the Earth. Mass is rebuilt from the two tank series and the stage and fairing flags, as the payload carries them. Engines: the vacuum thrust (thrust_vac_N) over the stage's full vacuum thrust, which sets the plume and the propellant flow; the model's thrust is lower by p_amb A_e near the ground and is 0 N while that exceeds it.
