## 1. Purpose, rules and disclosure

**Purpose.** Fix every structural and drive coefficient SP7 uses, with a central value, a range, a
source and a status, so that S2 can write the structure files without further research and so that
no value is chosen after an SP7 result is seen.

**Rules this note follows.**

1. Every number has a source (title, organisation or authors, year, URL or NTRS id, page or
   table) and a status, or it is marked assumed with a one-line reason.
2. Status labels:
   - [opened]: the source was downloaded or fetched and the passage read (by survey 06 or 07, by
     this step, or both; "re-read" means this step read it again).
   - [quoted]: the number was seen only as quoted by another document or a search summary.
   - (derived): arithmetic on opened values, shown or reproducible from the note.
   - (measured from the figure): read by pixel from an opened figure; the method is stated.
   - (inferred): a reading not stated in any source.
   - assumed: no source; the reason is given.
3. No range is narrower than its source's own spread unless a reason is written beside it.
4. No source is invented. A source that could not be opened is named as such.
5. A quoted-only value may not set a range end by itself; it may set a central, and it may enter
   an end together with opened values (for example as the model's own geometry). Where a quoted
   value lies near a range end or enters one, the row says so. Section 12 applies the same
   wording.
6. The planning probe's placeholders (survey 08: sigma_ult 450 MPa, p_u 3.5 bar, k_entry 20-100
   kg/MN, the "stiffened 2x/3x" forms, the 2 mm gauge) are not sources. Probe numbers appear only
   as labelled context.
7. Field names carry their units as the structure file will (`E_GPa`, `F_tu_MPa`, `t_min_mm`,
   `p_meop_bar`, `k_ts_kg_per_kN`, `axial_frequency_Hz`, `rise_time_s`); dimensionless fields
   carry no unit (`nu`, `eta_weld`, `*_fraction`, `*_ratio`). units.py converts GPa, MPa, mm, bar
   and kg/kN at the boundary (1 bar = 1e5 Pa). Section 12 lists the remaining field names.
8. Page numbers are PDF pages unless marked "printed".

**Disclosure.** The central values below were chosen after the planning probe (survey 08) had shown
which coefficients matter (the dynamic factor, the load-entry hardware, the buckling form, the tank
pressures), and the probe's one-at-a-time table also showed the direction and rough size of several
coefficients' effects on the added mass dm (sigma_ult, FS, p_u, the skirt length, E, t_min). The
review rounds' changes (sections 1.2 and 1.3 and the third round's revision section) were also
made with that table known. To keep that knowledge from steering the choice, each central value
follows a rule written beside it. The rules
used, the complete list: the opened value; the current edition's recommendation; the method as
written; the midpoint of the range or of the opened values; the median of the opened readings; a
flown tank's regulation-band bottom over its relief valve's top setting, where both are legible; the
one opened flight-hardware analogue, or the opened analogue nearest the case; the vehicle guide's
statement read plainly, with the one opened value of that kind; the value at a quoted temperature; a
quoted value; the Gerard row nearest the opened calibration, and that row's exponent for a member
no Gerard row describes; Wu's factor for the model's fidelity, with the opened calibration where
the model's base differs from the factor's; the population
average; no weld knockdown under a factor that already carries the weld lands; an inferred load path
or symmetry; SP1's shipped value; a fixed value (no range) where the vehicle's own source or the
analogue regulation names it and no opened source supports another; a user decision. No rule refers
to the value's effect on dm. Where a rule lands on a range end, the row says so and, where known,
which way dm moves at that end.

The range ends were also set with the probe's table known, and for a coefficient that moves dm
one way the band's ends are set by them, not by the centrals. Most ends are opened values, the
sources' spread (rule 3) or a bound derived from opened values (with the model's own geometry,
where the row says so). Section 1.4 lists every dm-lowering end or discrete alternative that
rests instead on an assumption, an analogue, a quoted value, a rule or a user decision (and every
such end whose direction is only expected or not monotone), and the fixed choices of that kind,
with its reason and the probe's one-at-a-time effect where the probe tested it. The
pre-registration (S6a) repeats this disclosure and section 1.4's table as they stand after the
last revision of this note (the rows added after the round-3 verification included).

### 1.4 The dm-lowering ends that rest on an assumption, an analogue, a rule or a decision

"End" means the end that lowers dm (or that lowers the row it belongs to); for a discrete
coefficient or a design row it is the alternative that lowers dm. The probe's figures are survey
08's one-at-a-time changes around its own placeholders (monocoque / stiffened 2x / stiffened
3x), context only (rule 6). The table lists every dm-lowering end or alternative that rests on an
assumption, an inference, a rule, a user decision or model output, or on an analogue, and every
end that rests on one of these whose direction is only expected or not monotone. An analogue,
here and in section 12 alike, is an opened value read beyond what it measures: another member's
value, another load case's or another law's, including an extrapolation, a law applied across
vehicles and a reading that needs an assumed parameter. Every dm-lowering end not listed is an
opened value used for what it measures (a material property, a design standard's or Wu's factor,
a Falcon 9 statement), an opened spread, a physical or validity limit, or a bound derived from
opened values, named in its row. Fixed choices (no range) are listed where they rest on an
assumption, an inference or an analogue and either a named alternative would raise dm in some case
or the choice moves dm both ways.

### 2.2 Stage-2 tank order

Not public, and the opened guides do not settle it. Read literally, two sentences of the 2015
guide disagree:

- the latch sentence: the stages are latched "between the top of the interstage and the base of
  the second-stage fuel tank" (FUG15 PDF p.12; the same in FUG25 printed p.9), which puts the fuel
  (RP-1) tank at the base of stage 2, the order of stage 1;
- the harness sentence: "At the top of the second-stage fuel tank the harnesses are routed across
  the payload attach fitting" (FUG15 PDF p.38), which puts the fuel tank at the top, under the
  payload attach fitting, the reverse order. The 2025 guide rewords it as "At the top of the second
  stage" (FUG25 printed p.28).

At least one of the two uses "fuel tank" for the stage-2 tank as a whole, so neither establishes
the order. Round 3's compliance review searched the web and found no public statement of it (a
search result, not a source). Round 2's reading, "inferred from FUG15 PDF p.12", rested on the
first sentence alone and is withdrawn.

Assumed: RP-1 below LOX, the order of stage 1, by the rule "the vehicle guide's statement read
plainly" applied to "a shorter version of the first-stage tank" using "most of the same materials,
construction, tooling and manufacturing techniques" (FUG15 PDF p.11; FUG25 printed p.9). The
structure file writes stage 2's `tank_order` with `assumed: true` and both sentences in its note,
never "public" or "inferred". The reverse order is not modelled; its direction on dm is not known,
and it acts only when the upper stack is sized (section 11). Also assumed from the same sentence:
a common dome in stage 2, and a central transfer tube to the MVac like stage 1's (under the
reverse order it would carry RP-1 through the LOX tank instead).

The stage-2 aft skirt length 0 does not depend on the order: under either reading the latches sit
at the base of the stage-2 tank, whatever that tank holds, so the interstage meets the tank's base
and stage 2 has no aft skirt in the load path (inferred from the latch sentence alone; fixed, not
ranged; written `assumed: true` with that reading in its note, section 12).

### 2.4 Stack lengths: a cross-check, and the bound behind the skirt and interstage ranges

Physical tank lengths, with the dome volumes and the 0.65 m tube counted (derived; a/b 4/3, dome
volume V_d = (2/3) pi r^3 / (a/b) = 9.627 m^3): LOX barrel L_LOX,phys = (V_LOX - 2 V_d) / (pi
r^2) = (237.23 - 2 x 9.627) / 10.521 = 20.72 m; RP-1 barrel L_RP1,phys = V_RP1 / (pi r^2 - pi
d_t^2 / 4) = 155.82 / (10.521 - 0.332) = 15.29 m (the aft dome adds what the common dome takes;
the tube displaces RP-1). The two barrels together are 36.01 m.

Where the interstage sits. Both guides say the interstage "is fixed to the forward end of the
first stage tank" (FUG15 PDF p.11; FUG25 printed p.8-9) [opened]. The usual arrangement, taken
here, joins it at the top of the LOX barrel, so the forward dome sits inside the interstage
(inferred). The alternative, an interstage starting above the forward-dome crown, adds the 1.37 m
dome depth below it.

The quoted 42.6 m stage length (WIKI-FT) states neither its datum nor whether it includes the 4.5 m
interstage or the engine nozzles below the skirt. Readings of the skirt length, at the central
coefficients (derived):

| Reading | Dome inside the interstage | Interstage above the crown |
|---|---|---|
| 42.6 m excludes the interstage, from the skirt's lower edge | 42.6 - 36.01 = 6.59 m | 5.22 m |
| 42.6 m includes the interstage | 6.59 - 4.5 = 2.09 m | 0.72 m (shorter than the 1.37 m aft dome it must enclose: rejected) |
| 42.6 m includes nozzles below the skirt (not public) | shorter than the first row by their protrusion | the same |

The first row depends on the dome ratio through the LOX barrel: 7.20 m with hemispherical domes and
6.48 m at a/b sqrt(2) (derived).

A closure on the opened totals (FUG Table 2-1: 70 m overall "including both stages, interstage and
fairing"; a 13.2 m fairing, section 2.1): 42.6 + 4.5 + 12.6 + 13.2 = 72.9 m, which closes on 70 m
with a 2.9 m overlap, for example the MVac nozzle inside the interstage if the 12.6 m includes it
(inferred); 42.6 + 12.6 + 13.2 = 68.4 m leaves 1.6 m unexplained. The opened totals therefore favour
the reading that excludes the interstage (inferred). Survey 06 also saw "41.2 m without the
interstage" for Falcon 9 v1.1 in a search summary (SF101) [quoted]; it supports the same
convention and sets nothing.

So the quoted length implies a skirt of up to about 6.6 m, less the unknown nozzle protrusion.
That is context only: a quoted value sets no range end by itself (rule 5). Round 2 set the
skirt's high end there as an exception; round 3 withdraws it (the gate's G3).

A bound from the opened totals, at the central coefficients (this sets the skirt's high end and both
interstage ends, section 3.4). Both guides give 70 m overall "including both stages, interstage and
fairing" (Table 2-1) and a 13.2 m fairing [opened], so stage 1 (with any engine nozzles below its
skirt), the interstage and stage 2 take 56.8 m below the fairing. The model's own physical tank
lengths at the central coefficients are 20.72 + 15.29 m for stage 1 and 4.07 + 3.89 m for stage 2
(stage-2 LOX barrel (62.07 - 2 x 9.627) / 10.521; RP-1 barrel 40.75 / (10.521 - 0.035) with its
0.212 m tube), 43.97 m in all (derived). They are the geometry the ends must fit, not a source for
them, and they rest on two quoted inputs: the propellant masses of the vehicle file the model flies
(WIKI-FT citing Espace & Exploration No. 39 [quoted], section 2.3) and the density centrals, taken
at WIKI-FT's quoted load temperatures (section 3.4). Those quoted values enter the skirt's and the
interstage's high ends together with the opened 70 m and 13.2 m, as the model's own geometry, and
set neither by itself (rule 5). The interstage runs from the top of the stage-1 LOX barrel to the
stage-2 latch plane, so the stage-1 forward dome and the stage-2 aft dome are inside it (an
interstage starting above the forward-dome crown gives the same total, the dome then counting in
stage 1's length). Nothing is counted for the nozzle protrusion below the skirt, the MVac's length
below its dome, or any stage-2 structure above its LOX barrel (none is public; each is taken as
zero, so the result is an upper bound at these coefficients). The skirt and the interstage then
share at most 56.8 - 43.97 = 12.83 m.

Each length's high end is that budget less the other length's minimum at the same coefficients.
The interstage must enclose the two domes, 2 r / (a/b): 2.745 m at the central a/b 4/3. (Its
range's low end, 3.66 m, is that minimum at the deepest dome in the range, two hemispheres, so that
it encloses the domes at every dome ratio the search takes; section 3.4.) The skirt's minimum is
section 3.4's 2.0 m, an assumed value that holds at every dome ratio (the deepest aft dome with
room for the top of the thrust structure). So L_s <= 12.83 - 2.745 = 10.09 m and L_is <= 12.83 -
2.0 = 10.83 m (derived). Round 3 subtracted the hemispherical 3.66 m from a budget computed at a/b
4/3, which mixed two dome ratios and put the skirt's end at 9.17 m, below the bound at every
single dome ratio (V3-01/1). Taken at each dome ratio alone, with the other coefficients at
central, the skirt's bound is 10.39 m at a/b 1, 10.09 m at 4/3 and 10.03 m at sqrt(2) (derived).
The pair nearest the budget that the model's ranges admit, the skirt at 10.09 m with the
interstage at its low end of 3.66 m, is 0.92 m longer than the budget.

The budget moves with the other coefficients, from 7.7 to 16.4 m over their ranges, mostly through
the LOX density (derived). Like the cross-check above, the bound is taken at the central
coefficients and holds there only. At the ends that lengthen the tanks (LOX 1,141.2 and RP-1 785.0
kg/m^3, ullage 0.05, tube 0.90 m), which the high-mass sets are expected to take (each lengthens a
barrel or enlarges the tube; section 3.4), the tanks are 47.7-49.1 m long over the dome-ratio
range and the budget 7.7-9.1 m. The skirt at 10.09 m then exceeds what the budget leaves it (5.1-5.5
m, with the interstage at its minimum at the same dome ratio) by 4.6-5.0 m, at the headline too,
where stage 2 is not sized (derived). The model does not impose the shared budget on the sets or on
the pair; section 9.3 states what the sets then describe and section 11 the direction. The quoted
readings (a 6.59 m skirt and a 4.5 m interstage, 11.09 m together) lie inside the central budget.

For comparison, the model's cylinder-equivalent stack above the skirt to the forward-dome crown is
14.811 + 22.548 + 1.3725 = 38.73 m, 1.35 m longer than the physical one (37.38 m): neglecting the
dome volumes lengthens the LOX barrel by 1.83 m, while leaving out the tube makes the model's RP-1
barrel 0.48 m shorter than the physical one. The longer LOX barrel adds 21.0 m^2 (7.1%) to the LOX
tank's 294.5 m^2 of model wall (derived). The plausibility rule therefore uses the physical
lengths (section 7): L_LOX,phys / L_LOX = 0.919 and L_RP1,phys / L_RP1 = 1.033 at the central
coefficients (derived).

### 3.2 Tank pressures (four tanks)

Each of the four tanks (stage-1 LOX, stage-1 RP-1, stage-2 LOX, stage-2 RP-1) carries its own pair,
so these two rows are eight coefficients. Definition: the differential pressure across the wall
(inside minus outside), in bar, one value for every load case (pad, push and flight). `p_meop_bar`
is the ullage (top-of-tank) MEOP: the regulated pressure with its relief-valve tolerance, without
the acceleration head, which the model adds itself as rho n g0 h (design 4.2: p(z) = p_u + rho n g0
h(z)). STD-5001B's definition of MEOP (PDF p.10) also includes "vehicle acceleration"; it is cited
here only for the relief-valve tolerance, so S2 must not read `p_meop_bar` as a whole-tank MEOP and
add the head twice. `p_min_fraction` x `p_meop_bar` is likewise an ullage value. Most of the
analogues below are absolute pressures read in flight at altitude (the S-IC flight band, the S-II
and S-IVB values in psia). For a tank regulated in absolute terms the differential at sea level is
about 1 bar lower; for one regulated in gauge terms it is the same. Which applies to Falcon 9 is
not public. One value for both therefore has a mixed direction: if Falcon 9 regulates in absolute
terms, it overstates the relief in the push's compression (the push is at sea level; favourable to
the vehicle, it lowers dm) and the hoop pressure in the pad's and the push's cases (either way,
depending on which mode governs each station). The pressure enumeration spans part of it (section
11). The pair is transcribed as `p_meop_bar` and `p_min_fraction`, with p_min = `p_min_fraction` x
`p_meop_bar`, so p_min <= MEOP always (the design's pair rule, 4.2.6). If S2 prefers a `p_min_bar`
field, its derived central is 0.84 x 2.62 = 2.20 bar and its range must be computed from the pair,
not ranged independently. The ratio form confines p_min to 0.40-0.93 of each MEOP, a smaller box
than ranging p_min on its own under p_min <= p_u (design 4.2.6, 4.3: "per tank MEOP and minimum").
Reason: the opened band ratios bound p_min/MEOP, not p_min alone. It changes the design's pair, so
it is listed in section 13 (item 13).

Why one row serves all four tanks: no Falcon 9 value is public for any tank, and the opened
analogues by tank type overlap (LOX 1.24-2.83 bar regulated and relief settings up to 3.10 bar, fuel
1.67 bar in vacuum, LH2 1.93-2.34 bar regulated and relief settings up to 2.55 bar), so one range
that covers them all is wider, not narrower, than any per-tank range the analogues would give. Each
tank still has its own two coefficients in the search, so the shared range does not tie the tanks
together. The RP-1 tanks' low end therefore comes from a LOX tank (the S-IC LOX pad value, 1.24
bar). The only RP-1 analogue, the S-IC fuel tank's in-flight minimum, is 1.67 bar at altitude but
0.66 bar as a sea-level differential; the pair's lowest p_min (0.40 x 1.24 = 0.50 bar) covers both
readings. The S-IC LOX flight band read at sea level (0.23-0.37 bar differential) is rejected as a
reading: on the pad that tank was prepressurized to "a maximum of 18 psig" (1.24 bar, a
differential) until T-3 s, and the 18-20 psia band is the in-flight regulation (SA503 PDF p.69), so
the band read at sea level is not the pad's differential. The pair cannot reach that reading (its
lowest MEOP is 1.24 bar and its lowest p_min 0.50 bar), and need not.

## 5. Breakdowns

The breakdown gives the structural mass above each station (design 4.2: m_s,above comes from the
breakdown, not from the sized thicknesses) and feeds the plausibility check. It is computed once at
the central coefficients and stored as fixed quantities (`<element>_kg`, section 12). Rules used for
every vehicle (assumed unless sourced):

- Engines: 9 x 470 kg, the Merlin 1D dry mass [quoted] (WIKI-M, citing Tom Mueller, Quora, 8 Jun
  2015; the engine version is not stated).
- Thrust structure: `k_ts_kg_per_kN` central x vacuum thrust (0.306 x 8,226.9 kN = 2,517 kg;
  Akin's 0.255 gives 2,098 kg as a cross-check). This uses k_ts as an average for placement only:
  on stage 1 the mass sits at the base, where it loads no barrel station, so its split from the
  base remainder moves neither dm nor any station load; only the printed remainder depends on it.
  On stage 2 the remainder sits at the forward end, so there the split moves the stage-2 station
  loads slightly (0.306 instead of round 1's 0.4125 moves 105 kg from the base to the forward
  end).
- Interstage: CAS12 Table 15, PDF p.83 (printed 65), lower-stage interstage, M = k_SM k1 S (3.2808
  D)^k2 with k1 7.7165, k2 0.4856, k_SM 0.7 (composite; 1.0 for aluminium), S = pi D L_is lateral
  area (m^2), D = 3.66 m, L_is = 4.5 m: 934 kg (aluminium 1,335 kg) [opened], re-read. In SI (no
  feet in the file): M_kg = k_SM x 13.740 x S_m2 x D_m^0.4856 (13.740 = 7.7165 x 3.2808^0.4856;
  derived); at D = 3.66 m and k_SM 0.7 that is 207.7 kg per metre of interstage length (derived).
  The same page says interstages are "jettisoned together with the lower of the two stages"; the
  increment's rule is section 5.5.
- Tanks: Heineman's relation, M = 10.41 V^0.75 (lbm, ft^3; tanks and insulation only; WU24 PDF
  p.24-25, from Heineman JSC-26098, 1994, not opened), applied to each stage's combined LOX + RP-1
  volume at the central coefficients. The chart (WU24 PDF p.24) plots stages with a common
  bulkhead (Saturn S-II, S-IVB, Centaur) as single points of combined volume and the separate-tank
  S-IC as two points; Falcon 9 has a common dome (FUG), so the combined volume follows the chart's
  convention. In SI: M_kg = 68.404 V_m3^0.75 (derived). The mass is split over the two barrels and
  the three domes by wall area; the common dome is counted once.
- Upper equipment: 1,000 kg at the top of stage 1 (assumed): the grid fins and the separation
  hardware sit there (FUG), but no mass is public. The size is an allowance of about 4.5% of the
  stage-1 dry mass. It is not ranged: 1 t is about 0.7% of the axial load at the top LOX station
  and 0.2% at the RP-1 stations (derived: about 141 t and 432 t above them), and its direction is
  stated in section 5.4.
- The rest (aft skirt, engine section, legs if included, feed and pressurization systems, thrust
  vector control, avionics, wiring, the transfer tube) is the remainder that closes the dry mass,
  placed at the base (assumed). Whether the 22.2 t includes legs and fins is not stated (survey
  06 section 2).
- MVac: 656 kg (assumed): the quoted Merlin 1D mass scaled by Akin's engine relation (AKIN16 slide
  25: M = 7.81e-4 T + 3.37e-5 T sqrt(Ae/At) + 59, T in N), whose ratio for MVac (981 kN, 165:1,
  FUG) to Merlin 1D (914.1 kN, the vehicle file's spacex.com value; 16:1 [quoted, WIKI-M]) is

- Stage-2 rest: the remainder, placed at the forward end (assumed: avionics and the payload

station's axial load in every case. Mass at the base loads no barrel station; the aft skirt carries

Effect on dm of where the remainder sits (inferred, approximate): mass moved from the base to the
top adds to a barrel station's envelope load at the governing envelope case's n (MECO's 5.195 g0
at the upper stations, with the LOX gone) and to its push load at n_peak (with the LOX on board).
While n_peak is below the governing envelope n (the headline: about 4.4 g0 at the central dynamic
factor), more top mass raises t_env more than t_push and lowers dm slightly (survey 08 found the top
mass moves dm by at most about 60 kg). When n_peak is above it (the step row and the 50 m stroke
at about 7.0 g0, and any case with a dynamic factor above about 1.40), more top mass can raise dm;
the concave buckling laws (t growing as N^0.5 to N^0.6) put the crossover somewhat above 5.195 g0.
The same holds for stage 2's forward-end remainder when stage 2 is sized. The 1,000 kg top share
is below survey 08's 2.0 t placeholder.

### 5.5 The interstage increment (rule for S2)

Design 4.2.4 sizes the interstage as m_is ((N_d,push / N_d,env)^n - 1)+ "with the Gerard
exponent". The rule, fixed now (section 13, item 14):

- m_is: Castellini's relation (above) at the case's `interstage_length_m`, 207.7 kg per metre
  (934 kg at the central 4.5 m). The breakdown's `interstage_kg` stays at its central value for
  the station loads, and the increment's own inertia is not added (design 4.2).
- The load: the axial load through the interstage is the upper stack's, U n g0 (stage 2 wet, the
  fairing if attached, the payload flown), the quantity the `structure_upper_stack_exceeded` flag
  compares (design 4.2.2). N_d,push uses n_peak (with the dynamic factor). N_d,env is the largest
  U n g0 over the pad's load cases while the interstage is on the vehicle (HOLD and the stage-1
  burn), times (1 + margin), with the pad's stage-1 n capped when the case sets `envelope_cap_g`
  (design 4.3). FS_u cancels in the ratio. The interstage's own mass and the upper equipment are
  left out of N: like U, they are constant from the hold to staging, so they would move both
  loads alike (stated).
- The exponent: `interstage_exponent` 0.6, fixed, `assumed: true`: the central Gerard row's
  exponent (the rule "that row's exponent for a member no Gerard row describes"; both
  ring-stiffened rows share it). The interstage is a composite honeycomb sandwich (FUG15 PDF
  p.11), which no opened Gerard row describes, and the plate-limited row's exponent 1 rests on
  LOV10's 2195 orthogrid plate limit, which does not describe a sandwich; so the exponent does not
  follow the active `gerard_row`. A larger exponent raises the increment: at the step row's ratio
  (n_peak 7.0 against MECO's 5.195 g0, 1.347), n = 1 gives 1.77 times the n = 0.6 value (0.347
  against 0.196; derived).
- No factor (section 4's table): the relation is fitted to as-built masses.
- The stage it is charged to: stage 1, proposed. The interstage is stage-1 hardware: it "is fixed
  to the forward end of the first-stage tank" and the separation system is at its forward end
  (FUG15 PDF p.11), Castellini's relation jettisons it with the lower stage (CAS12 PDF p.83), and
  the breakdown places its 934 kg in stage 1's 22.2 t (section 5.1). D-SP7-11's wording ("stage 2's

  charging it to stage 2; design 4.2.4 names only "the stage-2 increment". Charged to stage 2 it
  would be carried to orbit and cost about 24 kg of offload per kg (design 4.2.4), against about
  4.4 kg on stage 1 (survey 03: 4.38 kg of offload per kg of stage-1 dry mass at the headline).
  The choice goes to the user before S2 (section 13, item 14); S2 transcribes whichever the entry
  records. The stage-2 tanks' increment stays on stage 2 (design 4.2.4).
- Size, as an illustration: at the step row, the central coefficients and margin 0, 934 x 0.196

The rule, fixed now:

- M_model = the envelope masses of the stage-1 tank elements (forward dome, LOX barrel, common dome,
  RP-1 barrel, aft dome; not the aft skirt, ring or tube), each station or element times its
  factor, at the central coefficients. The factor is chosen by the envelope's governing mode at
  each station (M_model is built from envelope thicknesses): hoop, combined or monocoque take
  `nof_barrel`; a Gerard row or the plate-limited row takes `nof_stiffened`; domes take `nof_dome`.
  (Section 4's table chooses the increment's factor by the push's governing mode; the two can
  differ at one station.)
- M_model is computed on the physical tank geometry of section 2.4, so that the model's
  cylinder-equivalent lengths do not inflate it: each barrel's summed envelope mass is multiplied by
  L_phys / L_model, with L_LOX,phys = (V_LOX - 2 V_d) / (pi r^2) and
  L_RP1,phys = V_RP1 / (pi r^2 - pi d_t^2 / 4), at the central coefficients (0.919 and 1.033 for
  the gate; derived); the domes are unchanged. The cylinder-equivalent value is printed beside; the
  rule acts on the physical one.
- R = Heineman's relation on the stage's combined LOX + RP-1 volume at the same central volumes
  (6,038 kg for the gate; 5,870 kg for the README-loads fork).
- If M_model < 0.7 R (gate: 4,227 kg; fork: 4,109 kg), the physics band's adverse (high-mass) end
  dm is multiplied by 0.7 R / M_model.
- If M_model > 1.3 R (gate: 7,850 kg; fork: 7,631 kg), the band's favourable (low-mass) end dm is
  multiplied by 1.3 R / M_model.
- Otherwise nothing changes. The table, the factor and the per-tank R (7,152 kg gate, 6,952 kg
  fork) are printed in the Structure subsection and the findings note.

Caveats:

- The relation's own scatter exceeds its stated band, in both directions: the SLWT LH2 tank came
  out 35% below it (WU24 PDF p.25), while the only LOX/RP-1 points on the chart, the S-IC's two
  tanks, sit about 10% and 25% above the average curve (table above). For LOX/RP-1 tanks the
  relation may therefore under-predict, which would make M_model > 1.3 R, and so the favourable-end
  widening, more likely without the model being wrong. Akin's relations give 19% less than
  Heineman's combined-volume value for these tanks (31% less than the per-tank sum).
- The combined-volume R is 15.6% below the per-tank sum (x0.844). It lowers the 1.3 R ceiling
  from 9,298 to 7,850 kg, and whenever the rule fires on both conventions it cuts the favourable
  end 15.6% deeper (the multiplier 1.3 R / M_model is 15.6% smaller).
- M_model is still biased high after the physical-length correction: the model's base skin is
  13-26% thicker than the base the factors were measured on (section 3.1, with an assumed proof
  ratio). Survey 06 and the probe both found a monocoque LOX barrel alone near Heineman's per-tank
  LOX value. If M_model comes out above 1.3 R, the favourable end widens.

latitude and azimuth, 9.772092 m/s^2 per S05, recomputed in code); a vertical track (sin phi = 1);
a cold start; no carriage unless stated.

Shared kinematics. A net acceleration a(t) = a_p min(1, t/t_r) from rest gives, once the ramp has
ended, v^2 = 2 a_p s - a_p^2 t_r^2 / 12. Reaching v_e at L:

    a_p = [L - sqrt(L^2 - v_e^2 t_r^2 / 12)] / (t_r^2 / 12)    (the smaller root)

valid while the ramp ends inside the stroke (a_p t_r^2 / 6 < L and a_p t_r / 2 < v_e). This is the
design's plateau a' (4.2.1). At the inputs above it is about 29.51 m/s^2 (3.009 g0; derived as an
illustration, S6b recomputes it).

So, within S1's free closed-ring model, bending-only sizing under-sizes the ring at h/b above about
8.4 at 8 pads, 6.9 at 6 and 4.9 at 4 (3.7 at 3), and is adequate below. Direction: it lowers the
ring row's mass, and so dm, where the search takes the ring there: the physics band's low-mass set

move the check either way: the skirt joined along the ring's edge restrains its twist, which lowers
the ring's torsion but moves that moment into the skirt's edge; and the ring is curved (r/h 2.8 to
10.4 over the axis and range), which the straight-section stresses ignore. The span-to-depth ratio
runs from 3.6 (8 pads, h/b 12) to 10.2 (4 pads, h/b 1), so the beam theory is least reliable at the
deep sections where the check fails. Section 13, item 15 proposes sizing the section to the larger
of bending and this check.

The pad line (V3-05/1). S1 puts the pad reactions and the skirt's uniform reaction on one circle:
the pads are assumed to lie on the skirt's circle, a radial offset e = 0 between the pad line and
the skirt wall (the pad layout is a facility choice and is not public). An offset puts a uniformly
distributed twisting moment q e on the closed ring, which the ring carries as uniform ring-rolling
bending, M = q e r (the equilibrium of a half ring: the distributed moment's resultant, 2 q e r, is
balanced by the moment M at each of the two cuts), about the same axis as the pad moment S1 sizes.
With one sign of e it adds to the moment at the pads, with the other to the opposite-signed moment
at the midspans. At 8 pads the pad moment is q r^2 (1 - alpha cot alpha) = 0.0519 q r^2 = 0.174 q (r
= 1.83 m) and the midspan moment 0.0262 q r^2, so an offset of 0.095 m, about twice the central
section's width of 0.0505 m (depth 0.328 m), doubles the peak moment if it adds at the pads (the
ring x1.59 in mass) and raises it 1.50 times if it adds at the midspans (x1.31); an offset of the
second sign below 0.047 m lowers it, by at most 25% (at 0.024 m). At 4 pads the moments are larger
and the offset that matches the pad moment is 0.39 m (derived). Not modelled; the direction depends
on the sign and size of e, and dm rises for any offset beyond the small range of one sign (section
1.4).

Not credited (direction mixed or favourable to the vehicle): the cryogenic strength and stiffness
gain; the higher compression modulus (11.4 Msi) in buckling; structural damping (SP-8055 caps
design damping at 1% of critical, PDF p.25-26, printed 21-22); drag in the envelope (leaving it
out charges about 0.01% more, S05); the pad envelope's own bending. Falcon 9's tanks were
qualified for max-Q and ground-wind load cases (SX09), and preliminary sizing adds the compressive
line load of the bending moment to the axial load (LOV10 PDF p.3: "the sum of an applied axial
load and the maximum compressive contribution of an applied bending moment"). The envelope here is
axial only, so leaving out the max-Q and ground-wind bending lowers t_env and raises dm, possibly
much at the upper stations; a conservative omission whose size is not known.

Provenance, one transcription rule. Every range quantity `{central, low, high, source | assumed:
true, note}` (design 4.3) is written `assumed: true` whenever any of its three numbers is assumed,
inferred, set by a rule with no opened value behind it (as `eta_weld`'s 1.00; a midpoint or median
of opened values is arithmetic on them), a user decision, an analogue (section 1.4's definition: an
opened value read beyond what it measures, as another member's value, another load case's or another
law's, including an extrapolation, a law applied across vehicles and a reading that needs an assumed
parameter), or rests on a quoted value (which may set a central, and may enter an end only together
with opened values, never by itself: rule 5); its `note` then names which number is which and the
opened sources of the others (with pages). Only a quantity whose three numbers all rest on opened
sources used for what they measure, or on arithmetic on them (a bound derived from opened values
included), carries `source` alone.

Fixed values follow the same rule (V3-05/2). A fixed value that rests on an opened source used for
what it measures is a `Quantity` with `source`: `FS_ult`, the 2219-T851 pair, `radius_m`, stage
1's `tank_order` and `common_dome`, the Heineman, interstage and Akin constants, the payload limits
and `delta_gamma_table`. One that rests on an assumption, an inference, an analogue or a quoted
value is a `Quantity` with `assumed: true` and the reading in its `note`: `stations_per_barrel`
and `interstage_exponent` (assumed); `fitting_factor` (an analogue regulation, 14 CFR 25.625, with
the ring assumed wrought; written with `source` in round 3, changed under the one analogue
definition); stage 2's `aft_skirt_length_m` 0 (inferred from the latch sentence alone, FUG15 PDF
p.12; section 2.2); `lox_mass_fraction` (from the vehicle file's propellant masses, WIKI-FT citing
Espace & Exploration No. 39 [quoted]; the fork's fractions assumed equal to the gate's);

Wikipedia values [quoted], with the quoted mixture fractions, which nearly cancel); and the

## 13. Corrections to the approved design (for the phase file, before S2)

These change a number or a method the design fixed. Each needs a phase-file entry before S2
transcribes this note (D-SP7-31); items 1, 2, 6, 10, 11, 12 and 15 also touch code S1
committed (a3bd183, src/launchsim/structure.py).

1. Non-optimum factors by base (design 4.2, "barrels and skirt central 1.8 (1.54-1.9)"): a new
   design axis `nof_stiffened` (1.61; 1.45-1.90) multiplies the increments at stations sized by a
   Gerard row or the plate-limited row; `nof_barrel` (1.80; 1.54-1.90) keeps the skin-based modes;
   the factor is chosen per station by the governing push mode for the RP-1 stations (section 4's
   table; every LOX station takes `nof_barrel`, with the double count stated there). Against the
   design at the Gerard-sized stations: central -11%, low end -6%, high end equal.
2. A new design axis `nof_entry_ratio` (1.47; 1.05-1.47) multiplies the aft skirt's and the ring
   frame's factors (design 4.2.3 gave the ring the barrels' factor alone): at central the skirt
   takes 2.37 (1.61 x 1.47; 2.32 in round 2) and the ring 2.65 (1.80 x 1.47).
3. `nof_dome` range 1.50-2.36 (design 4.2: 1.28-2.76), central 1.90 unchanged.
4. `k_ts_kg_per_kN` range 0.197-0.628 (design 4.2.3: 0.20-0.36), with two questions for the entry.
   (a) The central rule: the note uses the median of the nine opened readings, 0.306, instead of
   the design's midpoint of the range (0.28 on the design's range; 0.4125 on this one, a value one
   extrapolated reading sets). The median counts nine readings of six relations; counted once
   per relation it is 0.2805, the design's 0.28, so the entry also decides the counting. (b)
   Whether the 0.628 reading, Castellini's Ref. 5 curve extrapolated 65% past its chart with
   unknown parameters, may set the high end; if not, the high end is 0.427, the Ref. 5 formula
   with SSM = 1, the g0 factor read as a number and the per-engine thrust, all assumed, and
   Falcon 9's engine mass, M_eng 470 kg [quoted], so a quoted value would enter that end (rule 5;
   survey 06 calls the g0 factor's role "interpretation uncertain"; read without it, the formula
   gives 0.033-0.044 kg/kN, section 4). The note keeps 0.628, as the adverse end.
5. `FS_ult` fixed at 1.40 and `fitting_factor` fixed at 1.15 (design 4.2.6 lists FS_u and the
   fitting factor among the ranged physics coefficients). Fixing FS_ult raises the physics band's
   low-mass end (1.25 out) and lowers its high-mass end (survey 06's 1.5 out).
6. The Gerard rows: `ring_common_y` and the two "improved" rows leave the band by the 30%
   tolerance against LOV10's constraint-satisfying (PANDA2) designs (section 3.3); the
   plate-limited row is anchored at the central row's envelope thickness taken before the t_min
   floor (design 4.2: "anchored at the envelope load").
7. The search (design 4.2.6): the stage-2-only five fixed for the physics sets at the step row
   (search B); the outer envelope as the search at the design axes' two corners (search C, the
   dm-raising corner at t_r = 1/(pi f)); the reduction rules and fallbacks of section 9.3, whose
   last step puts the wall-time budget to the user, not to a session decision.
8. lm_cmd's caps (design 4.6 and 4.10): 1.10 x the full-load need, fixed now.
9. The plausibility rule (refinements of D-SP7-33 and 4.2): Heineman's relation on the combined
   stage volume, which lowers both thresholds by 15.6% and, whenever the favourable-end rule fires
   on both conventions, cuts the favourable end 15.6% deeper; M_model on the physical tank lengths,
   each station's factor by its envelope mode; the ullage fraction defined over the liquid.
10. A new discrete physics coefficient `dome_alloy` (2219-T851 central, 2195 alternative; design
    4.3 has one materials block for every element).
11. The ring frame: S1 implemented a closed ring on N_p supports, M = q r^2 (1 - alpha cot alpha),
    where design 4.2.3 wrote the beam form q l^2/12 (S1's moment is 1.0% higher at 8 pads and
    4.4% higher at 4); `ring_pads` is an integer, so its break-even values are reported on whole
    counts (section 9.3 D).
12. The Gerard rows' home: design 4.3 puts "the Gerard rows" in the structure file's `buckling`
    block; S1 holds them as module constants (structure.GERARD_ROWS). The note proposes one source
    of truth, S1's, with the file naming rows only and a test against section 3.3's table.
13. The pressure pair (design 4.2.6, "each tank's (p_u, p_min) pair (p_min <= p_u)"; 4.3, "per
    tank MEOP and minimum"): ranged as (`p_meop_bar`, `p_min_fraction`), with p_min =
    `p_min_fraction` x `p_meop_bar`. This confines each tank's p_min to 0.40-0.93 of its MEOP, a
    smaller box than ranging p_min on its own under p_min <= p_u. Reason: the opened band ratios
    are upper bounds on p_min/MEOP, so the ratio is what the sources bound (section 3.2).
    `p_meop_bar` is defined as the ullage MEOP, without the acceleration head the model adds.
14. The interstage increment (design 4.2.4 and D-SP7-11; section 5.5): (a) the exponent fixed at
    the central Gerard row's 0.6 (`interstage_exponent`, assumed), not the active `gerard_row`'s,
    because the composite sandwich has no row; (b) the load, margin and cap as section 5.5 states;
    (c) the stage the increment is charged to: the note proposes stage 1, where the interstage is
    (FUG15 PDF p.11; it leaves with stage 1), against D-SP7-11's wording, which reads as stage 2.
    D-SP7-11 is a user answer, so this item goes to the user. Stage 1 costs about 4.4 kg of
    offload per kg of increment, stage 2 about 24 kg.
15. The ring frame's torsion and shear (design 4.2.3 sizes bending only; section 11): the note
    proposes that the section's width b be the larger of S1's bending b and the smallest b at
    which the combined check passes (torsion plus transverse shear at the neutral axis over F_tu /
    sqrt(3), and von Mises over the span and the section, at ultimate), a change to
    structure.ring_frame_mass_kg or a wrapper S2 adds. It raises the ring's mass where the check
    binds (x1.21 at 8 pads and h/b 12; x1.16 at 4 pads and h/b 6.5) and makes `ring_h_over_b`
    non-monotone (section 9.2). If the entry declines it, S2 prints the check's ratio beside every
    ring row, and the bias of section 11 (bending-only sizing lowers dm at deep sections and few
    pads) is stated wherever the ring row is reported. A per-pad term (each pad's local material
    and fitting, a total that does not fall with N_p) is not proposed; without it the `ring_pads`
    axis stops at 8 (section 4).
